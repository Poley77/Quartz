# The Strategy That Will Fix Health Care

**Summary**: Michael Porter and Thomas Lee's foundational 2013 HBR argument for outcome-based care — the clearest articulation of why the U.S. healthcare system fails and what the structural fix looks like.

**Sources**: Porter & Lee, Harvard Business Review, ["The Strategy That Will Fix Health Care"](https://hbr.org/2013/10/the-strategy-that-will-fix-health-care) (October 2013)

**Last updated**: 2026-06-22

---

## The Central Argument

Porter and Lee argue that the U.S. healthcare system's problems — high cost, poor outcomes, fragmentation, waste — all stem from a single root cause: **providers don't compete on value**. They compete on volume, on service lines, on amenity, on reputation. But almost never on who actually delivers better health outcomes per dollar spent.

The fix is not more regulation, more insurance coverage, or more spending. It is restructuring how providers compete — around **patient value**, defined as health outcomes per dollar of cost.

## The Six-Part Agenda

Porter and Lee lay out six interconnected changes needed to make value-based competition possible:

**1. Organize around medical conditions, not specialties or procedures.** Most health systems are organized by department (cardiology, orthopedics, oncology). This fragments care across the patient journey. Instead, providers should create **Integrated Practice Units (IPUs)** — multidisciplinary teams organized around a full cycle of care for a specific condition, from diagnosis through treatment through ongoing management.

**2. Measure outcomes and costs for every patient.** You cannot improve what you do not measure. Systematically tracking outcomes (not just process measures) and the full cost of delivering care across the patient journey is the foundation of value improvement.

**3. Move to bundled payments for care cycles.** [[fee-for-service]] pays for each service separately, fragmenting accountability. Bundled payments hold a single entity accountable for the full cycle of care for a condition — creating the right incentives for coordination, efficiency, and outcomes improvement.

**4. Integrate care across separate facilities.** The current system pushes patients across disconnected sites — hospital, specialist, rehab, primary care — with no entity accountable for the handoffs. High-value care requires integration across these boundaries.

**5. Expand geographic reach.** Excellent providers should treat more patients, not just local ones. Geographic concentration of expertise in specialized units allows for the volume and learning curves that drive quality improvement.

**6. Build an enabling IT platform.** All of the above requires data: outcomes data, cost data, patient data across the care cycle. Healthcare IT needs to be rebuilt around measuring and enabling value, not just billing.

## Why This Matters in 2026

The Porter/Lee framework is 13 years old, but it remains the clearest theoretical foundation for what [[value-based-care]] is trying to accomplish. Many of the programs that followed — ACOs, Medicare Advantage risk adjustment, bundled payment pilots, Integrated Practice Units — map directly to this agenda.

The framework is also useful diagnostically: when VBC programs underperform, it's often because they've implemented one or two elements (shared savings, some quality metrics) without the full structural change (IPUs, outcome measurement, cost transparency) that makes the system actually work.
