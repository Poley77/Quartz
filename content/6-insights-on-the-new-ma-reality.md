# 6 Insights on the New MA Reality

**Summary**: How the Medicare Advantage market is shifting after CMS rate cuts, tighter oversight, and rising medical costs — and what it means for plans, providers, and the VBC ecosystem.

**Sources**: Navina, "6 Insights on the New MA Reality" (2024–2025)

**Last updated**: 2026-06-22

---

## Background: What Changed

For most of its history, [[medicare-advantage-policy-primer|Medicare Advantage]] was a growth story. Enrollment climbed, margins were healthy, and plans competed aggressively for beneficiaries with rich supplemental benefits.

Starting in 2023–2024, several forces converged to disrupt this:

- CMS implemented rate-setting changes that reduced payment growth below plan expectations
- Medical cost trends accelerated post-COVID, particularly in utilization of deferred care
- CMS tightened prior authorization rules, reducing a key cost-management lever
- Increased RADV audit activity created revenue uncertainty around [[risk-adjustment-coding|risk adjustment]]

The result: MA profitability deteriorated sharply. Major insurers reported significant earnings misses in their MA segments. Several plans exited markets or cut supplemental benefits.

## Insight 1: The Easy Growth Era Is Over

Plans can no longer grow enrollment, maintain rich benefits, and generate strong margins simultaneously. The low-hanging fruit — enrolling healthier beneficiaries in low-cost markets — has largely been captured. Growth now requires competing on operational efficiency and care quality rather than benefit richness.

## Insight 2: Medical Cost Trends Are the Central Challenge

Utilization returned to and exceeded pre-COVID levels in 2023–2024, particularly for elective procedures that had been deferred during the pandemic. Plans that underestimated this trend in their bids saw costs come in substantially above projections. Accurate medical cost forecasting has become a core competitive competency.

## Insight 3: Risk Adjustment Is Under Scrutiny

CMS's transition to the V28 HCC model, combined with increased RADV auditing, is reducing the value of aggressive coding programs that were previously a significant source of MA revenue. Plans that relied heavily on retrospective coding (chart reviews, in-home assessments after the fact) are facing payment risk. The shift favors organizations with strong **prospective** coding — capturing accurate diagnoses at the point of care through clinical workflows rather than retrospective review.

This is directly relevant to the role of tools like Navina's AI, which focus on surfacing [[risk-adjustment-coding|coding opportunities]] at the time of the clinical encounter rather than after the fact.

## Insight 4: Prior Authorization Reform Creates Operational Pressure

CMS's new prior authorization rules require MA plans to respond faster and provide clearer criteria for denials. This reduces plans' ability to use PA as a broad cost-management tool and requires investment in utilization management infrastructure that can operate at speed and with greater transparency.

## Insight 5: Supplemental Benefits Are Being Cut

As margins compress, plans are reducing or eliminating supplemental benefits — dental, vision, OTC allowances, transportation — that drove MA enrollment growth. This creates a risk: if MA's value proposition relative to traditional Medicare narrows, enrollment growth could slow or reverse.

## Insight 6: Strong Clinical Performance Becomes Differentiating

In a tighter payment environment, plans that genuinely keep members healthy — reducing hospitalizations, managing chronic conditions proactively, maintaining high Star Ratings — have a structural advantage. Quality bonus payments become more important to margin. This shifts competitive advantage toward organizations with strong [[value-based-care|VBC]] capabilities rather than those primarily winning on benefit richness.
