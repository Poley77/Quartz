# Affordable Care Act (ACA)

**Summary**: The 2010 health reform law that created ACA Marketplaces, expanded Medicaid, and established consumer protections for private insurance. Still the foundation of U.S. coverage policy, though undergoing significant changes in 2025–2026.

**Sources**: `The Affordable Care Act 101.md`, `Understanding the Affordable Care Act.md`, `Eight Trends Shaping 2026 Healthcare Costs.md`, `What to expect in US healthcare in 2026 and beyond.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-05-12

---

## What the ACA Did

Signed by President Obama on March 23, 2010, the ACA was the largest restructuring of U.S. health insurance since [[medicare|Medicare and Medicaid]] in 1965. It built on the existing system rather than replacing it. Three main pillars:

**1. Medicaid expansion**
- Extended Medicaid eligibility to all adults up to 138% of the Federal Poverty Level (FPL)
- Made optional by a 2012 Supreme Court ruling; as of 2025, 40 states + DC have expanded
- The federal government covers 90% of expansion costs

**2. Health Insurance Marketplaces (Exchanges)**
- Online platforms where individuals and small businesses can compare and buy ACA-compliant plans
- Premium tax credits (subsidies) available on a sliding income scale
- 28 states use the federal platform (HealthCare.gov); others operate their own

**3. Employer mandate**
- Large employers (50+ full-time employees) must offer affordable, minimum-value coverage or pay penalties
- "Affordable" = employee self-only premium no more than 9.02% of household income (2025)

## Consumer Protections

The ACA established insurance market rules that remain in effect:
- **Guaranteed issue**: Insurers must sell to all applicants regardless of health status
- **No pre-existing condition exclusions**: Insurers cannot deny coverage or charge higher premiums based on health history
- **No lifetime or annual limits** on essential health benefits
- **Children to age 26** can stay on parents' insurance
- **Preventive services without cost-sharing**: Vaccinations, screenings, and other preventive care covered at no out-of-pocket cost
- **Medical Loss Ratio**: Insurers must spend at least 80–85% of premiums on medical care; excess goes back as rebates
- **Rate review**: Significant premium increases must be publicly justified

## Essential Health Benefits (EHBs)

All ACA-compliant plans must cover 10 categories:
1. Ambulatory patient services
2. Emergency services
3. Hospitalization
4. Pregnancy, maternity, and newborn care
5. Mental health and substance use disorder services
6. Prescription drugs
7. Rehabilitative and habilitative services
8. Laboratory services
9. Preventive and wellness services
10. Pediatric services (including oral and vision)

## How Marketplace Subsidies Work

**Premium Tax Credit (PTC)**: Reduces monthly premium payments. The subsidy is calculated as the difference between the benchmark plan cost and the enrollee's "required contribution" on a sliding income scale:

| Income (% FPL) | Required contribution (% of income) — 2025 |
|---|---|
| < 150% | 0% |
| 150–200% | 0–2% |
| 200–250% | 2–4% |
| 250–300% | 4–6% |
| 300–400% | 6–8.5% |
| 400%+ | 8.5% |

The enhanced subsidy structure (ARPA 2021, extended by IRA through 2025) eliminated the prior "subsidy cliff" at 400% FPL. **These enhanced subsidies are set to expire at the end of 2025**, reverting to the original ACA structure absent congressional action.

**Cost-Sharing Reductions (CSRs)**: For people with incomes 100–250% FPL, silver plans are modified to reduce deductibles and out-of-pocket costs. The most generous CSR (94% actuarial value, for 100–150% FPL) brings the average deductible from ~$5,000 to ~$87 (2025). CSR subsidies are only available in silver plans.

**Metal levels**: Bronze (lowest premium, highest OOP), Silver, Gold, Platinum (highest premium, lowest OOP). PTCs can be applied to any metal level; CSRs require silver.

## ACA Enrollment Trends

- Uninsured rate fell from 14–16% (pre-ACA) to a record low of **7.7%** (2023)
- ACA Marketplace enrollment: reached **20.8 million** in early 2024 (record)
- Overall individual market (on- and off-exchange): **25.2 million** in early 2025
- In 2026, ACA enrollment dropped by **more than 1 million** people due to enhanced subsidy expiration and OBBBA changes

## Major Changes Since 2010

| Year | Change |
|---|---|
| 2012 | Supreme Court makes Medicaid expansion optional |
| 2017 | IRS stops enforcing individual mandate; repeal/replace fails in Senate |
| 2019 | Individual mandate penalty reduced to $0 (Tax Cuts and Jobs Act) |
| 2018 | CSR payments to insurers ended by Trump admin; insurers raise silver premiums ("silver loading") |
| 2021 | ARPA enhances subsidies, eliminates 400% FPL cliff |
| 2022 | IRA extends enhanced subsidies through 2025 |
| 2022 | Biden admin fixes "family glitch" — family coverage affordability threshold changed |
| 2024 | Biden admin limits short-term non-compliant plans |
| 2025 | OBBBA changes auto-renewal, eligibility verification, and immigrant eligibility; enhanced subsidies expired |

## Current Challenges (2026)

- **Enhanced subsidy expiration**: With the ARPA/IRA subsidies lapsed, millions of enrollees face significantly higher premiums. Some states are developing state-funded subsidies to offset the impact.
- **OBBBA Marketplace changes**: New eligibility verification procedures, end of auto-renewals, repayment limits on PTC removed. CBO estimates ~2 million additional people become uninsured from these ACA-specific changes.
- **ACA Marketplace EBITDA**: Managed care EBITDA for the ACA segment expected to decline 25–30% through 2027 before stabilizing as payers adjust pricing.
- **Navigator funding cut**: CMS reduced funding for enrollment assistors from $100M to $10M starting 2026.
- **DACA recipients**: A November 2024 Biden rule allowing DACA recipients to enroll in ACA Marketplace plans was reversed by the Marketplace Integrity and Affordability Rule (June 2025).

The ACA does **not** have an expiration date; its future depends on legislation, regulation, and legal challenges.
