# Breaking Down the Care Platform Landscape

**Summary**: Who MSOs and care platform operators are, how they make money, and how they fit into the broader VBC ecosystem.

**Sources**: Workweek, "Breaking Down the Care Platform Landscape" (May 2022)

**Last updated**: 2026-06-22

---

## What Is a Care Platform?

"Care platform" is a loose term for companies that have vertically integrated multiple functions required to succeed in [[value-based-care]] — typically combining some mix of primary care delivery, data analytics, care management, and payer contracting under one roof.

These organizations exist because VBC is operationally complex. Succeeding in risk-based contracts requires capabilities that most independent physician practices don't have: population health data, 24/7 care management teams, actuarial expertise, compliance infrastructure. Care platforms and MSOs exist to provide these capabilities at scale.

## MSOs: Management Services Organizations

An MSO (Management Services Organization) is a non-clinical company that contracts with physician practices to provide back-office and operational support. In the VBC context, an MSO typically provides:

- **Data and analytics**: Risk stratification, care gap identification, quality metric tracking
- **Care management**: Nurse care coordinators, patient outreach, chronic disease management programs
- **Coding and revenue cycle**: [[risk-adjustment-coding|HCC coding]] support, claims submission, revenue optimization
- **Payer contracting**: Negotiating and managing risk-based contracts on behalf of the physician network
- **Compliance**: Regulatory compliance, quality reporting, ACO participation management

The physician practice retains clinical independence — physicians make clinical decisions. The MSO handles everything else.

## How MSOs Make Money

MSOs typically earn revenue in one or more of the following ways:

**Percentage of shared savings**: The MSO takes a percentage of any shared savings the physician network earns from VBC contracts. Aligned incentives — if the physicians do well, the MSO does well.

**PMPM fees**: A fixed per-member per-month fee for services provided to the physician network's patient panel.

**Equity participation**: Some MSOs take equity stakes in the physician practices they support, participating in long-term value creation as the practice grows.

The MSO model has attracted significant private equity investment because it creates a scalable, recurring-revenue business sitting on top of the VBC transition.

## Key Players in the Landscape

The care platform landscape includes several distinct archetypes:

**Pure-play MSOs**: Organizations like Aledade and Privia that work exclusively with independent primary care practices, providing VBC infrastructure without owning or employing the physicians.

**Payer-provider hybrids**: Organizations like ChenMed, Oak Street Health (now CVS), and Iora Health (now One Medical/Amazon) that both deliver primary care and participate in risk-based contracts — capturing margin at both the care delivery and insurance layers.

**Tech-enabled care platforms**: Organizations that lead with technology (data, AI, analytics) and add care management services on top. These include companies like Navina, Arcadia, and Innovaccer operating at the data layer of the [[inside-the-value-based-care-ecosystem|VBC ecosystem]].

**Retail entrants**: CVS, Walgreens, and Amazon entering primary care delivery with the explicit intent of participating in VBC contracts. Their distribution scale and data assets give them structural advantages if they can execute on care quality.

## Why This Landscape Is Consolidating

The MSO and care platform space has seen rapid consolidation because:

1. Scale matters — larger patient panels provide more statistical stability in risk contracts and more leverage in payer negotiations
2. Private equity sees the VBC transition as a multi-decade opportunity and has been aggressively rolling up physician practices and MSOs
3. Payers themselves (UnitedHealth/Optum, CVS/Aetna, Humana) have been acquiring care delivery assets to capture more of the value chain
