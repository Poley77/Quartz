## Financial Condition

Large healthcare systems are operating under extraordinary financial strain:
- Many major health systems operate on margins as low as **1–2%**, leaving virtually no buffer for unexpected expenses
- General acute hospital EBITDA margins: estimated at 6.8% in 2024, with recovery to 7.6% by 2029 — but this recovery will be uneven
- Hospitals' share of overall provider profits is expected to decline from **41% (2019) to 38% (2029)** as care shifts to lower-cost outpatient settings

**Near-term headwinds (2025–2027)**:
- Tariff impacts on medical supplies and equipment
- Expiration of ACA enhanced subsidies → more uninsured → more uncompensated care
- [[obbba|OBBBA]] Medicaid changes → reduced Medicaid reimbursement + increased uncompensated care
- Proposed site neutrality policies (would equalize Medicare payments regardless of care setting)

## Uncompensated Care

Hospitals must treat all patients regardless of ability to pay, particularly in emergency departments. As Medicaid and ACA enrollment shrinks from OBBBA changes:
- Uncompensated care is expected to rise significantly in 2027
- This will pressure EBITDA margins by reducing net reimbursement per patient
- Safety-net hospitals and those serving high-Medicaid populations are most exposed

## The Shift Away from Inpatient Care

A structural shift is underway from inpatient hospital settings to lower-cost sites:
- **Ambulatory surgery centers (ASCs)**: Gaining volume from newly approved orthopedic and cardiovascular procedures; EBITDA margins ~24% (2024)
- **Urgent care centers**: Growing steadily, EBITDA margins ~15%
- **Home health and hospice**: Growing rapidly (6% and 9% EBITDA growth annually 2024–2029, respectively) due to aging population and care preference
- **Virtual care**: Telehealth expanded during COVID-19 and is now a baseline expectation, though reimbursement remains uncertain

More than half of patient encounters now occur in outpatient settings rather than traditional inpatient hospitals.

## Capital and Infrastructure Challenges

- Hospital buildings, medical equipment, and technology all require constant investment
- Tight operating margins make it difficult to generate capital for essential upgrades
- Hospitals face a dual burden: expanding into ambulatory and community settings (patient preference) while maintaining expensive inpatient infrastructure
- Competition for commercially insured patients from retail chains (CVS MinuteClinic, Amazon Clinic), technology companies, and private equity-backed entities

## The "Medical Arms Race" (Historical Context)

A dynamic documented as far back as the 1980s: because physicians admit patients to hospitals and hospitals must attract physicians, hospitals have historically competed by purchasing expensive equipment. This creates redundancy and drives up costs. The [[value-based-care|shift to value-based care]] and capitated payment is intended to reduce this dynamic, but it is slow.

## AI and Technology

Hospitals are significant adopters of [[ai-and-technology|AI tools]] for:
- Ambient AI scribing (reducing documentation time 60–70%)
- Clinical decision support and diagnostic assistance
- Predictive analytics for patient flow and readmission risk
- Revenue cycle management and prior authorization automation
- Operational efficiency (OR scheduling, supply chain)

Most major health systems use Epic Systems or Oracle Health (Cerner) as their EHR foundation; AI tools must integrate with these platforms to see adoption.
