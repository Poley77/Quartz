# PHYX 2025 Report: Navina's AI for Value-Based Care

**Summary**: Key findings from an independent study measuring the real-world impact of Navina's AI on clinical workflows, coding accuracy, and care gap closure in value-based care settings.

**Sources**: PHYX / Navina, "AI for Value-Based Care: Independent Study Shows Navina's Impact" (2025)

**Last updated**: 2026-06-22

---

## What This Study Is

PHYX conducted an independent evaluation of Navina's AI platform across a cohort of primary care physicians operating in [[value-based-care]] arrangements — primarily [[medicare-advantage-policy-primer|Medicare Advantage]] and ACO settings. The study was designed to measure whether AI-assisted clinical review actually changes physician behavior and improves measurable outcomes.

This type of independent validation matters in the current market: many AI health tools claim to improve care and coding, but few have been rigorously evaluated outside of vendor-produced case studies.

## What Navina Does

Navina's platform aggregates and analyzes patient data from multiple sources — EHRs, claims, labs, prior visit notes — and surfaces actionable insights at the point of care. The primary use cases relevant to VBC are:

**Care gap closure**: Identifying patients who are overdue for preventive screenings, chronic disease monitoring, or follow-up care — and surfacing these gaps for the physician before or during the encounter.

**[[risk-adjustment-coding|HCC coding support]]**: Identifying chronic conditions documented in the patient's history that are relevant to the current visit and should be addressed and re-documented to maintain accurate risk scores.

**Clinical summarization**: Synthesizing complex patient histories — multiple conditions, specialist notes, prior hospitalizations — into a concise clinical summary that reduces time-to-context for the physician.

## Key Findings

**Coding accuracy improved**: Physicians using Navina documented more HCC-relevant conditions per patient visit than those using standard EHR workflows. The increase reflected genuine clinical documentation of real conditions — not upcoding. Retrospective chart review confirmed the conditions were present in the patient record.

**Care gaps closed at higher rates**: Patients seen by Navina-assisted physicians had higher rates of preventive care completion — screenings, vaccinations, A1C monitoring — compared to baseline. The platform's pre-visit summaries prompted physicians to address gaps that might otherwise be overlooked in a time-pressured encounter.

**Physician time efficiency**: Physicians reported spending less time on chart review and more time on direct patient interaction. This is significant in primary care, where cognitive overload and administrative burden are leading drivers of [[workforce|burnout]].

**Financial impact**: For a typical primary care practice in a capitated VBC arrangement, the combination of improved coding accuracy and care gap closure translated to measurable per-member revenue improvement and quality metric gains.

## Why This Matters

The study validates a broader thesis about the role of [[ai-and-technology|AI in healthcare]]: the highest-value AI applications in the near term are not autonomous decision-making systems, but tools that help clinicians work through the information they already have more quickly and completely.

In a VBC context specifically, the financial and quality stakes of each patient encounter are higher than under FFS — accurate coding and care gap closure directly affect revenue and Star Ratings. AI tools that systematically improve performance on these dimensions at the point of care have a compelling value proposition.

The PHYX study also demonstrates that independent validation matters. As AI health tools proliferate, organizations selecting platforms should look for real-world evidence of impact across diverse patient populations, not just vendor-reported metrics.
