# AI and Technology in Healthcare

**Summary**: Artificial intelligence has moved from pilot project to operational infrastructure in U.S. healthcare — with ambient scribing already reaching 10%+ of physicians and generative AI creating both cost-saving opportunities and new risks like coding inflation.

**Sources**: `What to expect in US healthcare in 2026 and beyond.md`, `Healthcare System Priorities in 2026.md`, `10 Healthcare Trends Reshaping the US Healthcare in 2026.md`, `Eight Trends Shaping 2026 Healthcare Costs.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-05-12

---

## The Shift from Pilot to Infrastructure

As of 2024, **85% of healthcare organizations** surveyed by McKinsey are pursuing generative AI initiatives or have already implemented solutions. Among those implementing gen AI, 61% intend to partner with vendors to develop customized solutions. AI is no longer a future technology in healthcare — it is current operational reality for leading organizations.

## Key Applications

### Ambient Scribing (Most Mature)
AI ambient scribing tools listen to patient-clinician conversations and automatically generate clinical notes:
- Currently used by an estimated **10%+ of U.S. physicians**
- Demonstrated **60–70% reduction in documentation time** in early implementations
- Directly addresses [[workforce|clinician burnout]] — one of the most urgent operational challenges
- Rapidly adopted after appearing in the late 2010s; now being scaled enterprise-wide at major health systems

### Clinical Decision Support and Diagnostics
- AI analyzes medical images, lab results, and clinical data to flag potential diagnoses
- Particularly impactful in radiology and pathology (high-volume, pattern-recognition tasks)
- Functions as a safety net and efficiency multiplier, not a replacement for clinical judgment

### Predictive Analytics
- Identifies patients at high risk for hospital readmission, ED visits, or disease progression
- Enables proactive care management and resource deployment before expensive acute events
- Essential for success in [[value-based-care|value-based care]] arrangements where health systems bear financial risk for outcomes

### Administrative Automation
- Prior authorization processing (predicting which services require auth and initiating requests automatically)
- Claims coding and submission
- Insurance eligibility verification
- Revenue cycle management
- Appointment scheduling and patient routing

### Patient Engagement
- AI chatbots and virtual assistants for routine inquiries, medication reminders, and symptom triage
- 24/7 access without staff involvement
- Routing patients to appropriate care settings (urgent care vs. ED vs. telehealth)

## The AI Coding Risk

A significant concern in 2026: AI documentation and coding tools are increasing the proportion of visits coded at **higher complexity levels**, which in turn increases billing amounts — independent of the actual health status of the patient population. Payers (Blue Cross Blue Shield and others) are finding that AI-driven documentation is raising billing amounts, and are deploying their own AI-based utilization management and payment controls in response. This dynamic is expected to put **upward pressure on total healthcare spending**.

## Health Services and Technology (HST) Sector

HST — software platforms, data analytics, tech-enabled services — is the **fastest-growing segment** of the healthcare industry:
- Estimated 8% annual revenue growth and 9% EBITDA growth (2024–2029)
- HST EBITDA projected to exceed $110 billion by 2029 (McKinsey)
- Top 25 HST players represent 29% of total HST revenue (up from 20% in 2019)
- $11.9 billion in venture capital investment in HST as of Q3 2025

Four drivers of growth:
1. Gen AI adoption for workflow-heavy solutions
2. Payer and provider outsourcing as margin pressure drives efficiency-seeking
3. Shift in value pools from services to software platforms
4. Consolidation of large HST players owned by providers or payers

## Interoperability

Data interoperability has advanced substantially by 2026 through regulatory requirements and industry adoption of FHIR standards. Healthcare organizations are beginning to realize tangible benefits from enhanced data transfer across providers, payers, and care settings. Interoperability is now treated as an infrastructure requirement, not a future aspiration. The [[obbba|OBBBA's]] Rural Health Transformation Program explicitly funds interoperable EHR systems and telehealth infrastructure.

## Cybersecurity

As digital transformation accelerates, cybersecurity risks have escalated across the healthcare system. Healthcare is a high-value target for ransomware and data breaches:
- Organizations are implementing zero-trust infrastructure and continuous monitoring
- Data governance frameworks are expanding to cover AI ethics and algorithmic transparency
- Data breaches trigger mandatory reporting, potential fines, class-action lawsuits, and severe reputational damage
- Cybersecurity has become a **board-level strategic priority** rather than a purely IT issue

## Barriers to AI Adoption

- **Data standardization**: Inconsistent FHIR adoption means data from different sources cannot always be correctly interpreted
- **Siloed systems**: Healthcare organizations run numerous disparate applications that don't communicate
- **Legacy infrastructure**: Older systems lacking modern APIs create longer implementation cycles
- **EHR integration**: Most major health systems use Epic Systems or Oracle Health (Cerner); AI tools must integrate natively with these platforms to see adoption
- **Proof of ROI**: In a 1–2% margin environment, CFOs require demonstrated financial return before committing to AI investments
