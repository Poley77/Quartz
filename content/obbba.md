# One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA)

**Summary**: The 2025 federal budget reconciliation law that made the largest cuts to Medicaid in the program's history, restructured ACA subsidies, and created a $50 billion Rural Health Transformation Program. Estimated to leave ~10 million more Americans uninsured.

**Sources**: [Eight Trends Shaping 2026 Healthcare Costs](https://www.healthsystemtracker.org/chart-collection/eight-trends-shaping-2026-healthcare-costs/#Average%20monthly%20health%20insurance%20premiums,%20by%20market%20segment,%202010-2024), [What to expect in US healthcare in 2026 and beyond](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/healthcare/our-insights/what-to-expect-in-us-healthcare), [The Affordable Care Act 101](https://www.kff.org/affordable-care-act/health-policy-101-the-affordable-care-act/)

**Last updated**: 2026-05-12

## What It Is

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act is the 2025 budget reconciliation legislation passed by the Republican-controlled Congress and signed by President Trump. It is the most consequential healthcare legislation since the [[affordable-care-act|ACA]]. The CBO estimates it will reduce federal Medicaid spending by over **$900 billion** over a decade and result in approximately **10 million additional uninsured Americans**.

## Medicaid Provisions

### Work/Community Engagement Requirements
Starting in 2027, approximately **20 million people** made eligible for Medicaid through the ACA expansion must verify they are:
- Working 80 hours/month, OR
- Enrolled in education or job training, OR
- Engaged in other qualifying activities, OR
- Exempt (caregivers, people with disabilities, etc.)

Failure to verify compliance triggers disenrollment. Implementation requires states to build new verification systems and conduct more frequent eligibility redeterminations — significant administrative costs with no direct care value.

### Provider Tax and Payment Changes
- States **cannot create new provider taxes** or increase existing taxes used to generate Medicaid matching funds
- Existing provider taxes are phased down in expansion states
- State-directed payments to hospitals and nursing facilities are **capped at 100% of Medicare rates** for expansion states and 110% for non-expansion states — reducing provider revenue significantly given that many Medicaid-directed payments currently exceed these caps

### Enrollment Impact
McKinsey estimates managed care organizations will lose **7 million Medicaid members** in 2027–28 from these changes alone (above and beyond the ~2–3 million already lost post-pandemic). CBO projects the 6–7 million disenrollments will push managed Medicaid EBITDA to **approximately negative $7 billion by 2028**.

## ACA Marketplace Provisions

The OBBBA also changes [[affordable-care-act|ACA Marketplace]] operations:
- **Ends automatic enrollment renewals**: Enrollees must actively re-enroll
- **New eligibility verification procedures**: More stringent income and immigration status checks
- **Removes repayment limits on premium tax credits**: If income exceeds estimates at year-end, full repayment of excess subsidies is required
- **Immigrant eligibility changes**: Restricts premium tax credit access for certain lawfully present immigrants
- CBO estimates these ACA changes will result in **~2 million additional uninsured**

## Rural Health Transformation Program

A notable provision added to address concerns about rural healthcare impacts of Medicaid cuts:
- **$50 billion allocated to states over 5 fiscal years** (2026–2030)
- Year 1 (2026): $10 billion distributed, ranging from $147M to $281M per state
- Half allocated equally by state; half by CMS based on rural population share, rural facility density, and CMS discretion
- Funds may be used for: telehealth infrastructure, remote patient monitoring, EHR modernization, AI for clinical decision support, alternative payment models, mobile clinics

**Important limitations**:
- Rural Health Transformation funds are **temporary** and do not fully offset permanent Medicaid funding cuts in rural areas
- Payments to providers for care cannot exceed 15% of a state's total transformation fund award
- States must move quickly; there are deadlines and scoring criteria for additional funding

States receiving additional funds committed to policies such as interstate licensure compacts, expanded scope of practice (pharmacists, paramedics), and SNAP restrictions aligning with Trump administration health goals ("Make America Healthy Again" policies).

## Financial Impact on the Healthcare Industry

| Segment | Expected Impact |
|---|---|
| Medicaid managed care | EBITDA ~negative $7B by 2028; modest recovery in 2029 |
| ACA Marketplace payers | 25–30% EBITDA decline 2024–2027; recovery from 2028 |
| Hospitals | Increased uncompensated care; Medicaid provider rate cuts; site neutrality uncertainty |
| Group insurance (ESI) | Beneficiary as Medicaid disenrollees transition to employer plans |
| Health services & technology | $50B Rural Transformation creates funded tech deployment opportunities |

## Political Context

The OBBBA was passed on a party-line vote. Democrats opposed the Medicaid cuts and ACA changes. The CBO's projections of 10 million newly uninsured became a major flashpoint in the 2026 midterm elections.
