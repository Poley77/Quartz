# Healthcare Costs

**Summary**: The U.S. spends more on healthcare than any other nation — nearly one in every five dollars in the economy — and costs continue to grow despite persistent efforts at containment.

**Sources**: [Eight Trends Shaping 2026 Healthcare Costs](https://www.healthsystemtracker.org/chart-collection/eight-trends-shaping-2026-healthcare-costs/#Average%20monthly%20health%20insurance%20premiums,%20by%20market%20segment,%202010-2024), [What to expect in US healthcare in 2026 and beyond](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/healthcare/our-insights/what-to-expect-in-us-healthcare), Healthcare System Priorities in 2026, [The U.S. Health Care System in 2026: Inside the Great Healthcare Plan](https://www.biobrain.io/blog/the-u-s-health-care-system-in-2026-inside-the-great-healthcare-plan)

**Last updated**: 2026-05-12

## Scale

- Healthcare represents nearly **one-fifth of the U.S. GDP** — the highest share of any country
- Cost shared between federal government (31%), state/local government (16%), employers (18%), individuals out-of-pocket (6%), and other private payers
- The U.S. spends roughly **twice as much per capita on prescription drugs** as peer nations
- Industry EBITDA as a percentage of national health expenditure fell from 11.2% (2019) to 8.9% (2024), and is projected to reach 8.7% by 2027 (Source: McKinsey)

## Who Worries About It

Healthcare costs are the **top household financial worry** for Americans in 2026 — ranking above food, rent, and utilities (KFF Health Tracking Poll). Key consumer pain points:
- 61% express strong negative sentiment toward hospital bills and medical debt
- 60% report rising prescription drug prices as a significant burden
- 57% scrutinize insurance premiums and deductibles more closely than before
- 48% say healthcare contributes to broader financial strain
- 30% have reduced medical spending in the last six months

## Trends Driving Cost Growth

### Medical Cost Inflation
Medical costs rose an average of **7% annually** between 2021 and 2024. Pharmacy costs grew even faster at **9% annually**, largely due to GLP-1 drugs and specialty injectables. (Source: McKinsey)

### Drug Spending
- Total drug spending rose **11% in 2024**; GLP-1 agonists (Ozempic, Wegovy, etc.) accounted for roughly half the increase
- U.S. gross drug spending projected to reach **$990 billion by 2029**, growing ~8% annually
- See [[pharmacy]] for detail

### Aging Population
- The 80+ population is growing at 4.8% per year and costs $1,673 per member per month (vs. $972 for ages 70–74)
- Expected to add 0.5–1.0% to annual claims costs through 2029

### Hospital Spending
- Hospitals accounted for **40% of overall healthcare spending growth** in 2022–2024 (KFF)
- High-cost drugs (cell/gene therapies) administered in hospital outpatient departments inflate hospital costs
- Care shifting to ASCs and urgent care is beginning to offset this, but slowly

### AI and Coding Intensity
[[ai-and-technology|AI documentation tools]] are increasing the proportion of visits coded at higher complexity levels, raising average billing amounts independent of actual patient health changes. Payers are responding with their own AI-enabled utilization management. This dynamic is expected to put **upward pressure on healthcare spending** in 2026 and beyond.

### Premium Increases

- ACA Marketplace: with enhanced subsidies expiring, marketplace premiums rose significantly in 2026; more than 1 million people dropped coverage
- Employer-sponsored: private insurance costs expected to rise further in 2026; some insurers requested premium increases citing anticipated tariff-related drug cost increases
- A family of four with ESI paid $6,296 in premiums + $3,564 in OOP in 2023

## Why Costs Are Hard to Control

Several structural features of the U.S. system make cost containment difficult:

**Fee-for-service incentives**: Traditional FFS payment rewards volume. Ordering more tests, procedures, and visits increases revenue. See [[value-based-care]] for the shift underway.

**Administrative fragmentation**: Unlike single-payer systems, the U.S. has thousands of payers with different billing requirements, prior authorization processes, and formularies. Administrative overhead is estimated to cost hundreds of billions annually.

**Consolidation**: Provider markets have become highly concentrated. One or two health systems control all inpatient commercial care in half of U.S. metropolitan areas, giving them pricing power over insurers. See [[healthcare-consolidation]].

**Technology diffusion**: New technologies (GLP-1s, specialty biologics, cell/gene therapies) are rapidly adopted and extremely expensive. The U.S. system has few mechanisms to delay or limit adoption.

**Inappropriate utilization**: Studies have consistently found that 15–30% of some surgical procedures are inappropriate or of uncertain benefit (RAND studies). AI may help reduce this but also risks increasing coding-based billing.

## Policy Responses in 2026

- **Drug pricing**: Inflation Reduction Act enabled Medicare price negotiations on a limited drug list; Trump administration pursuing "most-favored nation" pricing through TrumpRx.gov; PBM reform in the Consolidated Appropriations Act of 2026
- **Price transparency**: Hospitals and health plans required to publish pricing; Great Healthcare Plan proposes extending to all Medicare/Medicaid providers
- **Medicaid cuts**: OBBBA reduces federal Medicaid spending by $900B+ over a decade
- **Site neutrality**: Proposed policies would equalize Medicare payments for the same service at hospital vs. outpatient settings — hospitals oppose this strongly
