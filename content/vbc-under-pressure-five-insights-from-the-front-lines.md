# VBC Under Pressure: Five Insights from the Front Lines

**Summary**: Practitioner-level observations on why implementing value-based care is harder than the theory suggests — the real operational, financial, and data challenges organizations face.

**Sources**: Navina, "VBC Under Pressure: Five Insights from the Front Lines" (2025)

**Last updated**: 2026-06-22

---

## Overview

The shift to [[value-based-care]] looks clean on paper: align incentives, share risk, keep patients healthy. In practice, organizations making the transition face compounding pressures that don't appear in policy white papers. This page captures five practitioner insights on where the real friction lives.

## Insight 1: The Data Problem Is Worse Than Expected

VBC requires knowing which patients are high-risk before they get sick. But most health systems are still building the underlying data infrastructure — claims feeds, EHR integrations, care gap registries — that population health management requires.

Even organizations with good data often struggle with **data timeliness**: claims data can lag 60–90 days, meaning by the time you know a patient had a hospitalization, the opportunity to intervene has passed. Real-time clinical data, where available, is fragmented across systems.

## Insight 2: Dual Running Costs Are Crushing Margins

During the transition from FFS to VBC, organizations don't get to shut down one system while standing up the other. They run both simultaneously:

- FFS billing infrastructure (still needed for revenue today)
- VBC care management and analytics (needed to succeed tomorrow)

With hospital and physician practice margins running at 1–3%, there is very little financial runway to absorb these dual overhead costs. This is one reason the transition has been slower than CMS projected.

## Insight 3: Coding Accuracy Determines Financial Performance

Under VBC — especially in [[medicare|Medicare Advantage]] and capitated ACO models — payment is tied to the **risk score** of the patient population. Risk scores are derived from [[risk-adjustment-coding|HCC (Hierarchical Condition Category) codes]] documented in medical records.

If chronic conditions are underdocumented, the organization's risk score is too low, and their capitation payment doesn't reflect the true burden of illness in their population. This isn't fraud — it's a documentation gap. But it has real financial consequences. Organizations that invest in closing coding gaps can capture hundreds of dollars per member per year in additional revenue.

## Insight 4: Specialist Alignment Is a Structural Problem

[[value-based-care|VBC]] creates a direct tension within health systems: the organization has an incentive to reduce unnecessary specialist referrals and procedures, while specialist physicians are typically paid on FFS production. More referrals, more revenue for the specialist.

Resolving this requires compensation redesign — moving specialists toward shared savings or quality-based bonuses rather than pure volume. This is politically difficult inside health systems and takes years to implement.

## Insight 5: Patient Engagement Is the Last Mile

Even with the right data, the right care plans, and well-aligned providers, VBC success ultimately depends on patients actually showing up for care. Chronic disease management requires patients to:

- Fill prescriptions and adhere to medication regimens
- Attend preventive screenings and follow-up appointments
- Engage with care coordinators and health coaches

Populations in VBC arrangements often include historically underserved patients with significant social barriers to care — transportation, housing instability, language barriers. Addressing these [[health-equity|social determinants of health]] is increasingly recognized as essential to VBC performance, not a separate program.
